Astrophysics

A shadow across a star

Two tiny planets cross the center of the same star on circular, edge-on orbits. One orbits every 8 days, the other every 27. The inner planet’s transit lasts 64 minutes. Treat each planet as a point and each crossing as a straight line at mid-transit speed.

How long does the outer planet take to cross?

Show worked solution

96 minutes. Both shadows cross the same stellar diameter. Kepler’s third law gives orbital radius proportional to period to the power ⅔. Orbital speed is circumference divided by period, so transit time scales with the cube root of the period. Multiply 64 by the cube root of 27/8, or 3/2.

Quantum computing

Two fair qubits

Prepare the Bell pair (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2. A phase flip (Z) hits the first qubit in a quarter of otherwise ideal trials. Apply a Hadamard (H) to each qubit and measure both in the computational basis. Each bit on its own is fair.

How often do the two measurements match?

Show worked solution

3/4 of the trials. With no flip, the two Hadamards restore the original Bell pair and the measured strings are 00 or 11. A Z flip turns the plus sign into a minus. The two Hadamards then produce (|01⟩ + |10⟩)/√2. Every flipped trial yields opposite bits.

Mathematics

Race to three tosses

Toss a fair coin independently until HHT or HTH first appears in three consecutive tosses. Each pattern has probability 1/8 in a fixed three-toss window.

What is the chance that HHT appears first?

Show worked solution

2/3. Start at the first H. If the next toss is H, the next T seals an HHT win. If the next two tosses are TH, HTH wins. If they are TT, you are back to waiting for the first H. So one round yields an HHT win with probability 1/2, an HTH win with 1/4, and a restart with 1/4. Conditioning on a decisive round gives (1/2)/(3/4) = 2/3.

Which run would you ship?

Two fictional weather feeds overlap at midnight. Convert to UTC, expand N/S to NORTH/SOUTH, and merge identical reports for one station and instant. Reject conflicting readings for that pair. Keep simultaneous readings from different stations and sort by instant, then station. Times have minute precision.

January 7 · UTC+05:3005:28 · N · 4 °C05:30 · S · 7 °C
January 6–7 · UTCJan 6, 23:58 · NORTH · 4 °CJan 7, 00:00 · NORTH · 5 °C

All three runs return three rows totaling 16 °C. Which ordered UTC output would you ship?

Show worked solution

Ship B. Subtract 5 hours and 30 minutes from the local timestamps. The first local reading moves to January 6 at 23:58 UTC and matches the UTC feed exactly. At midnight NORTH reports 5 °C and SOUTH reports 7 °C. A shifts the earlier date a day forward. C gives each station the other station’s midnight reading.
